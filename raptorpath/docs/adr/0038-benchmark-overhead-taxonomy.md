# ADR-0038: Benchmark Overhead Taxonomy and Methodology

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-03-18

## Context

The transport comparison benchmark (ADR-0036) shows raptorpath with 38-50% "overhead" across all scenarios. This number is frequently misinterpreted as wire protocol inefficiency. In reality, the benchmark measures **FEC repair symbol overhead** exclusively — it bypasses wire protocol serialization entirely. The wire protocol has its own overhead sources (padding, headers) that are unmeasured by this benchmark.

This ADR documents exactly what the benchmark measures, what each metric means, and catalogs the five distinct layers of overhead in the protocol.

## Decision

### Benchmark Methodology

The transport comparison benchmark runs 6 scenarios × 5 transport configurations:

**Scenarios:** dc_low_loss, wifi_bursty, lte_high_rtt, wifi_lte_hetero, lossy_satellite, wifi_lte_asymmetric

**Transports:** quic_single, quic_dual_rr, quic_dual_minrtt, raptorpath_single, raptorpath_dual

Each configuration/scenario pair runs 20 trials of 4000 source symbols. Results are aggregated as mean ± stddev.

### Metric Definitions

| Metric | Formula | What it measures |
|--------|---------|------------------|
| `recovery_rate` | symbols recovered / symbols sent | Fraction of source data successfully delivered |
| `goodput_ratio` | symbols recovered / total transmissions | Useful data per unit of channel capacity consumed |
| `overhead_pct` | (total_transmissions - unique) / unique × 100 | Extra transmissions as a percentage of source symbols |
| `latency_p50/p95/p99` | percentile of (delivery_time - send_time) per symbol | End-to-end delivery latency at various percentiles |
| `completion_time` | max(delivery_time) - min(send_time) | Wall-clock time to deliver all symbols |
| `in_order_rate` | fraction of consecutive deliveries in sequence order | Measure of reordering in the delivery stream |

### Overhead Taxonomy — Five Layers

| Layer | Source | Magnitude | Measured by benchmark? |
|-------|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| 1. FEC repair symbols | Proactive repair from `FecRateController` | 38-50% (at 0-8% loss) | **Yes** — counted as `overhead_pct` |
| 2. Window-mode padding | 1 packet → 1 padded symbol (`frame_window_packet`) | Up to 68% per symbol (160B VoIP in 512B) | No — benchmark operates on fixed-size symbols directly |
| 3. Per-symbol metadata | `WireSymbol` bincode serialization (block_id, payload_id, seq, is_repair, data) | ~22B per symbol (4.3% at 512B) | No — benchmark doesn't serialize to wire |
| 4. Per-batch + wire header | `WIRE_MAGIC` (4B) + version (4B) + `SymbolBatch` fields (batch_seq, timestamp, path_id) | ~48B per batch (`WIRE_OVERHEAD`) | No |
| 5. FEC repair metadata | Backend-specific repair encoding info (RLC coefficients, METTLE bin map, streaming layer data) | 4-248B per repair symbol | No — benchmark uses in-memory symbol objects |

### How to Interpret Results

The benchmark compares **recovery strategies**, not wire efficiency:

- **QUIC overhead (0-10%)**: `ReliableSimChannel` re-enqueues dropped packets with an RTT delay penalty. Retransmissions are internal to the channel model and appear as increased latency, not explicit overhead symbols. The overhead column only counts additional transmissions beyond the 4000 source symbols.

- **Raptorpath overhead (38-50%)**: Repair symbols generated by the FEC rate controller are counted explicitly in `total_transmissions`. This is by design — the repair symbols are the mechanism that enables faster recovery than retransmission.

- **The meaningful comparison** is in the latency and recovery columns: raptorpath trades bandwidth (higher overhead) for lower tail latency (faster loss recovery without waiting for retransmission round-trips).

### Benchmark Artifacts

Two artifacts inflate the benchmark's overhead numbers beyond what production traffic would see:

1. **Repair floor bug (fixed in ADR-0039)**: Lines 655 and 811 in `transport_comparison_bench.rs` had `.max(1)` on the repair count calculation, forcing at least 1 repair per 10-symbol batch even when `repair_rate` was 0. At 0% loss, this created a 10% overhead floor that doesn't exist in the production `run_window_sender()` code (which uses the fractional repair accumulator from ADR-0037).

2. **PI controller wind-up**: The benchmark's `FecRateController` starts with default parameters that ramp up repair rate aggressively in early batches before ACK feedback arrives. The first ~20 batches (~200 symbols) generate excessive repair, biasing the overall overhead upward. In production, the fractional accumulator naturally handles this.

**Key observations:**
- Raptorpath wins **best p99 latency** in 5 of 6 scenarios (all except dc_low_loss) and ties recovery in all 6.
- Raptorpath_dual achieves **p99 = 200ms** on lossy_satellite vs QUIC's 600ms (3x improvement).
- Raptorpath delivers **100% in-order** in all scenarios (reorder buffer working), vs QUIC's 50-57%.
- QUIC wins goodput in all scenarios due to lower overhead (retransmissions vs proactive repair).
- The `.max(1)` fix (ADR-0039) did not reduce dc_low_loss overhead — **PI controller wind-up** is the dominant factor, ramping repair aggressively in early batches before the estimator converges.

## Consequences

- Benchmark results can now be interpreted correctly — the overhead column reflects FEC strategy cost, not protocol waste.
- The five-layer overhead taxonomy identifies optimization targets: layer 2 (window padding) has the largest unmeasured impact and is addressed in ADR-0039.
- The benchmark artifact documentation prevents future confusion about the repair floor and PI wind-up effects.
- Wire protocol overhead (layers 3-5) is documented but not benchmarked; a separate wire overhead benchmark would be needed to measure these.
